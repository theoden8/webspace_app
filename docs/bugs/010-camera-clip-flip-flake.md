# BUG-010 — Android camera probe reports one colour from the looping clip

Status: open

## Symptom

`Build Android` → `Run emulator integration scenarios` fails scenario 2 of
`per-site camera modes behave on a real Android WebView`
([integration_test/camera_test.dart](../../integration_test/camera_test.dart)):

```
second colour of the clip should appear; saw [[27, 204, 114]] after 30100ms
```

The probe gets a decoded frame and hundreds of samples, all of the clip's
first colour, so the tier reads a correctly playing virtual-camera stream as a
frozen one. Everything else on the job is green and master is green on the same
job, so it presents as an unrelated flake on whatever PR happens to be running.

## Root mechanism / invariant

The scenario's claim is "the clip is playing, not frozen on frame 0", and the
only evidence it can collect is a *change of colour* sampled off the synthetic
stream. That makes the assertion only as reliable as the share of playback time
the second colour occupies. The invariant: **each of the fixture's colours must
hold a constant, substantial fraction of the clip's media time, so that
observing the flip costs a bounded number of decoded frames and no timing
race.** A clip that spends almost all of its duration in one colour turns the
scenario into a coin flip against the emulator's decode rate, and no probe
window makes a race deterministic.

The fixture ([integration_test/fixtures/virtual_camera_video.dart](../../integration_test/fixtures/virtual_camera_video.dart))
is generated by [tool/generate_camera_video_fixture.js](../../tool/generate_camera_video_fixture.js).
Spec: CAM-010's "A video source plays and loops on-device" scenario in
[openspec/specs/web-camera-access/spec.md](../../openspec/specs/web-camera-access/spec.md).

## Fix attempts

1. **2026-08-16 — PR #532** (`ac117be`, "Wait for the clip to flip in the
   Android camera probe"). Replaced the fixed 4s sampling window with a wait
   for the first transition, capped at 30s, on the reading that the emulator's
   software decoder simply runs several times slower than real time (the same
   4s window had produced 8, 2 and 0 transitions across runs). *Why partial*:
   it treated a race as slowness. The clip carried its second colour on a
   single frame at the very end — measured on the committed fixture: two frames
   total, colour A at 0ms and colour B at 500ms of a 500.3ms clip — so a
   sampler had to catch B in the sliver between that frame's presentation and
   the loop wrap. Widening the window multiplies the number of attempts at a
   race with a low per-attempt probability; it does not remove the race, and
   the scenario failed again on PR #569 with zero transitions across ~300
   samples in 30s.

2. **2026-09-02 — PR #569 follow-up (this branch).** Fixed the generator, not
   the probe: the clip now alternates colour on *every* frame, so each colour
   holds half the media time and the first flip costs two decoded frames. The
   old generator painted the canvas twice and let `captureStream(10)` sample
   it, and that capture emits a frame only when the canvas is touched — hence
   the two-frame clip. It now captures with `captureStream(0)` +
   `track.requestFrame()`, one encoded frame per painted colour, and the
   generator decodes what it just encoded, walks the presented frames via
   `requestVideoFrameCallback`, and refuses to write a fixture whose first flip
   is not on frame 1. The property is re-derived from the committed bytes in
   CI by `the committed clip alternates colour on every frame` in
   [test/browser/camera_stream_real_engine.test.js](../../test/browser/camera_stream_real_engine.test.js)
   (fails on the old fixture, passes on this one). *Why partial*: see the open
   gap below — the assertion still bottoms out on the emulator decoding at
   least two frames inside the window, which is a far weaker assumption than
   before but not a guarantee.

## Known open gaps

- A runner that presents fewer than two frames of a 24fps clip in 30s still
  fails the scenario. That is now indistinguishable from a genuinely stalled
  stream at the probe's level; ruling it out would need a decode-rate signal
  from the WebView the probe does not have.
- The CI gate walks the clip under desktop Chromium. It proves what the
  committed bytes contain, not that the Android WebView presents them at the
  same rate, so a decoder-side regression on the emulator still surfaces only
  as a red emulator tier.
